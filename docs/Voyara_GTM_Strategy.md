# VoyVibe — Go-To-Market Strategy

*Prepared: June 2026*

---

## The Core Insight

Every Indian white collar family group trip has one person who does all the work and fourteen people who just show up. The organiser builds the plan in their head, coordinates on WhatsApp, and absorbs the blame when things go wrong. The others go along with the plan, quietly miss what mattered to them, and carry a low-grade regret they never voice.

VoyVibe doesn't replace that organiser. It makes them look good — and gives everyone else a way to be heard without starting an argument.

---

## Who Exactly We Are Building For

**Primary user: The Trip Organiser**
Indian professional, 30–50 years old, US/Canada/UK diaspora or urban India (Bengaluru, Mumbai, Delhi, Hyderabad, Pune). Has organised 2–4 group trips with friends or family. Currently managing the trip across WhatsApp, a half-finished Google Doc, and Splitwise. Dreads being asked "what's the plan for Day 3?" for the fifteenth time.

**Secondary user: The Quiet Participant**
Goes along with everything. Has a checklist — maybe one must-do place, a dietary requirement, a preference for starting late. Never asserts it because they don't want to create friction. Ends up with quiet regret when it doesn't happen.

**The group profile:**
- 2–4 families, 8–20 people
- Know each other well — college friends, colleague friend groups, long-time family friends
- Mix of vegetarian and non-vegetarian. At least one family doesn't drink. At least one has young kids.
- Budget-conscious but not cheap. Willing to pay for good experiences.
- WhatsApp is their operating system for everything.

## Positioning

**What VoyVibe is not:**
Not a travel booking app. Not an AI itinerary generator. Not a competitor to TripAdvisor or MakeMyTrip.

**What VoyVibe is:**
The one app that understands your group is not one person — it is five families with five sets of dietary needs, five different ideas of what "a good trip" means, and one person who somehow has to hold it all together.

**The one-line pitch:**
*"Everyone in the plan. No one left out."*

Alternative angles:
- *"The trip planner that knows your group — not just your destination."*
- *"Plan once. Everyone knows. No more WhatsApp chaos."*

## The Three Features That Will Drive Adoption

These are not the full product. These are the three things that will make someone tell a friend about VoyVibe.

**1. Pre-trip alignment questionnaire**
Before planning starts, every family answers five questions privately: When does your family wake up? What can't your family eat? What's the one thing someone in your family would regret missing? What's your rough daily budget comfort? Any accessibility needs?

The organiser sees a group compatibility summary: "3 of 4 families prefer starting after 9am. 2 families are vegetarian. Must-dos: Golden Gate Bridge (3 families), wine tasting (1 family). Budget: mostly mid-range, one family prefers budget options."

No debate. No group vote. Just clarity before the arguments can start.

**2. Vegetarian-aware restaurant discovery**
The Discover tab searches Google Places for restaurants filtered to the group's dietary profile. If 2 families are vegetarian, it surfaces veg-friendly restaurants first. If someone doesn't drink, alcohol-heavy bars don't dominate the list.

This is the "oh, this actually gets us" moment for this audience. No other travel app thinks about this.

**3. One-tap WhatsApp day plan**
The organiser taps one button and a clean, readable day plan goes into the group WhatsApp — activities with times, meeting points, rough costs. It looks professional. It doesn't require anyone else to download anything. Someone in the group will ask "what app is this?" and the organiser sends the link.

This is the viral loop. Every shared plan is a word-of-mouth acquisition.

## Channel Strategy

### Phase 1 — Seed (Months 1–3)
**Goal:** Get 20–50 real trips planned through VoyVibe. Collect feedback. Fix what breaks.

- Start with your own network. You know Indian families who travel. 5–10 organisers from personal connections who will give honest feedback.
- Offer to help plan their next trip together using VoyVibe. Be present. Watch where they struggle.
- Do not launch publicly yet. You want to understand real usage before you acquire strangers.

### Phase 2 — Community (Months 3–6)
**Goal:** 200–500 active users. Establish VoyVibe as the known tool in this community.

**Reddit:**
- r/ABCDesis — Indian Americans, very active, strong travel discussion threads
- r/IndianAmerican — family life, cultural topics, travel comes up regularly
- r/india and r/AskIndia — urban Indians planning domestic trips
- Approach: don't advertise. Post genuinely about the problem ("does anyone else find group trip planning with families exhausting?"). Build credibility. Mention VoyVibe when it's relevant.

**Facebook Groups:**
- Indian Parents Network (US/Canada)
- NRI groups specific to cities — Bay Area Desis, Toronto Indian Community, etc.
- Desi Moms/Dads groups — these are very active and travel discussions happen regularly
- These groups respond well to "I built this for our community" framing

**LinkedIn:**
- Indian professionals share tools that made their lives easier
- A post from the founder — "I built this because planning a group Airbnb trip with 4 Indian families broke my brain" — will resonate and get reshared
- This community values authenticity over slick marketing

**WhatsApp forward:**
- Ask your first 20 users to share the day plan into their group. The format does the marketing.

### Phase 3 — Content and Micro-influencers (Months 6–12)
**Goal:** 2,000–5,000 users. Sustainable word-of-mouth growth.

**Instagram/YouTube micro-influencers:**
- Indian family travel creators with 10,000–100,000 followers are more effective than large ones
- They have genuine trust with their audience
- Don't pay them. Give them early access and help them plan a real trip using VoyVibe. If the product is good, they'll post about it.
- Search: "NRI family travel," "Indian family Yellowstone," "desi road trip" — these creators exist and are undermonetised

**YouTube content:**
- "How I planned our 4-family trip to Yellowstone" — show the real workflow using VoyVibe
- This type of content has evergreen search value

**India-specific:**
- Urban India (Bengaluru, Mumbai, Pune, Delhi) — the same WhatsApp-forward strategy works even better
- The domestic travel boom (Goa, Manali, Rajasthan, Kerala) means organisers are actively looking for better tools
- Consider a Hindi/regional language interface for Phase 3

## The Viral Loop

```
Organiser uses VoyVibe to plan the trip
        ↓
Taps "Share to WhatsApp" — day plan goes into group chat
        ↓
Someone in the group asks "what app is that?"
        ↓
Organiser shares the link
        ↓
That person organises the next trip using VoyVibe
        ↓
Repeat
```

The WhatsApp share is not a feature. It is the entire distribution strategy. Every shared plan needs to look good, carry the VoyVibe name subtly, and be easy to explain in one sentence.

---

## What NOT to Do

- **Don't try to get all families on the app at launch.** The organiser is your user. Other families consume the output. Getting everyone to download an app is a losing strategy.
- **Don't compete on AI itinerary generation.** TripMind and Wonderlog are racing to be ChatGPT for travel. You will lose that race. Your edge is group intelligence, not generic AI.
- **Don't go broad.** Resisting the urge to build for everyone is the hardest discipline in product. Indian white collar group travel is specific enough to be winnable.
- **Don't over-invest in paid acquisition.** This community spreads things via trust networks, not ads. Spend the time on community, not Google Ads.

## Pricing

Given the goal is genuine usage over revenue maximisation:

**Free tier:** Manual trip planning, family profiles, basic expense tracking, WhatsApp day plan share. No limit.

**Paid tier (₹499 / $6.99 per trip or $49/year):**
- AI trip planner
- Vegetarian-aware restaurant discovery
- Pre-trip alignment questionnaire
- Expense transparency reports

The per-trip pricing fits the low-frequency use pattern. Users don't feel guilty about a subscription they use twice a year.

For India: ₹299–499 per trip is the right range for urban white collar. Not trivial, but clearly worth it.

## The Decision You Need to Make First

**Diaspora first or India first?**

| | Diaspora (US/Canada/UK) | India (Urban) |
|--|--|--|
| Market size | Smaller, higher income | Much larger, growing fast |
| Willingness to pay | Higher | Growing, more price-sensitive |
| WhatsApp virality | Strong | Extremely strong |
| Problem acuteness | High | Very high (dietary complexity greater) |
| Competition | TripMind, Wonderlog | MakeMyTrip (doesn't solve this) |
| Your familiarity | You are this user | Requires more research |

Recommendation: Start with diaspora because you are the user. You know the context, the culture, the pain personally. Validate the product with people you can talk to easily. Expand to India in Phase 3 when the product is proven.

## What Success Looks Like at Each Stage

**Month 3:** 50 real trips planned. At least 10 organisers who used it for a real trip with other families. Clear signal on which of the three core features people actually used.

**Month 6:** 500 users. The WhatsApp share is happening organically without prompting. At least one Reddit or Facebook post about VoyVibe that you didn't write.

**Month 12:** 3,000–5,000 users. Word-of-mouth is measurable — you can ask "how did you hear about us?" and the answer is usually "my friend shared it in our WhatsApp group." That is the product working.

---

## The Honest Caveat

This is a real problem with a real audience. The question of whether people will use it comes down to one thing: is the first experience good enough that the organiser tells someone else?

If someone plans a trip, shares the day plan to WhatsApp, and their friends say "this is so much better than what we usually do" — VoyVibe grows. If the setup is too complex, the AI plan is generic, or the WhatsApp share looks messy — they go back to Google Docs.

The product has to earn word-of-mouth. The GTM strategy just creates the conditions for that to happen.
