Splitwise Workflow

End-to-end guide: how costs flow from activity entry through group expense splitting to final settlement — covering cost modes, split strategies, balance calculation, and the settlement algorithm.

Overview
End-to-End Flow
Five stages from planning to settlement. Each stage is independently configurable per expense.
Stage 1
Cost Entry — AddActivityModal
When adding or editing an activity, you choose how to express the cost. The stored value is always costPerPerson — the three modes just change how it's calculated.
👤
Per Person
costPerPerson = input
Entry: $30/person
4 travellers → $120 total
👨‍👩‍👧
Per Family
costPerPerson = input ÷ avgFamilySize
Entry: $90/family, avg 3 members
→ $30/person stored
💰
Total Cost New
costPerPerson = input ÷ totalMembers
Entry: $900 total, 8 travellers
→ $112.50/person stored
Why always store costPerPerson? The itinerary uses it to calculate the total trip cost summary (cost × N members). When the itinerary is pushed to Splitwise, amount = costPerPerson × allMembers.length.
Stage 2
Push Itinerary to Splitwise
One tap converts all costed activities into expense records in the Splitwise ledger. Expenses link back to their source activity via activityId — so edits to the activity automatically update the expense.
Live sync: After pushing, editing an activity's cost automatically updates the linked expense (activityId is the FK). Marking an activity as "Skipped" sets excluded: true on its expense — the item is hidden from settlement but stays in the record.
Stage 3
Configure Each Expense
Expand any expense card to configure four things independently: include/skip, actual amount, split mode, and distribution type.
👤 Individual (By Person)
Cost split equally across participating members. You can toggle individual members in or out. Works across families.

Stage 3 · Detail
Custom (Uneven) Split
When families have different room sizes, different nights stayed, or any other reason the equal split doesn't fit — switch to Custom to enter exact amounts per participant.
Airbnb scenario: 3 families rent a $900 villa for 5 nights. Smith family takes the master suite ($400 worth), Jones take a double room ($300), Kim gets the studio ($200). Equal split ($300 each) would be unfair — custom split lets you enter the real amounts.
Remaining balance indicator: While entering custom amounts, a live counter shows how much is still unassigned (amber), over-allocated (red), or balanced (✅ green). Custom amounts are saved as exp.customShares keyed by famId (group mode) or memberId (individual mode).
Stage 4
Balance Calculation
For each member: net = total paid − total owed across all non-excluded expenses. Positive net means they're owed money; negative means they owe.
Example trip — 3 families, $1,430 total expenses. Alice (Smith) paid for the Airbnb ($900). Carol (Jones) paid for the boat tour ($280). Eve (Kim) paid for dinner ($250). Split mode: By Family (equal $143/family each on all except Airbnb which is custom).

Stage 5
Settlement — Who Pays Whom
The greedy algorithm minimises the number of transfers. It matches the largest creditor against the largest debtor, settles as much as possible, then moves on.
Algorithm — calcSettlements()
StepActionResult
1 Sort members: credits descending (Alice +$370), debts ascending (Carol −$150, Eve −$70) Two queues ready
2 Match Alice (+370) vs Carol (−150): transfer = min(370, 150) = $150 Alice net → +$220, Carol settled
3 Match Alice (+220) vs Eve (−70): transfer = min(220, 70) = $70 Alice net → +$150, Eve settled
4 Alice net = +$150 but no debtors remain — floating credit absorbed by rounding Done. 2 transfers only.
