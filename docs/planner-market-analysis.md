## 1. What "Plan with a Planner" actually is (3 monetization models)

The vision can be monetized three ways — they're not mutually exclusive, and the right answer is
likely a **hybrid that starts with one**:

| Model | How it makes money | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Advisor SaaS** — Voyara is the tool advisors use with clients | $/advisor/mo (+ tiers) | Recurring, high margin, easy to start | Small TAM alone; advisors are price-sensitive |
| **B. Marketplace take-rate** — match travelers ↔ planners, take a cut | % of planner fee or trip GMV | Scales with trip value; aligned incentives | Two-sided cold-start; trust/liability |
| **C. Consumer planning fee** — sell "a planned trip" as a service | flat fee / trip ($99–$499) | Simple consumer story | You carry supply (planners) + quality |

**Recommended sequence:** **A → B**. Land advisors with the best execution tool (the itinerary +
split + on-trip companion they don't have today), then layer the marketplace once you have supply +
demand liquidity. (C is a packaging of B.)

## 3. By the numbers — bottom-up (the model that matters)

Top-down TAM is a vanity number; here's the **bottom-up SOM** with every lever exposed.

### Model A — Advisor SaaS (the beachhead)
```
Addressable independent/host-agency advisors (global, group-leaning)   ~250,000–400,000   ◐
  (US alone ~100k+ advisors ⬤; Fora added ~5k+ since 2021; TravelJoy/Travefy/Tern serve tens of k)
Price (blended)                                                        ~$40–70 / mo      ◐ (TravelJoy ~$30–50)
→ Capture 1%   = 3,000 advisors × $55 × 12   ≈ $2.0 M ARR
→ Capture 5%   = 15,000 × $660               ≈ $9.9 M ARR
→ Capture 10%  = 30,000 × $660               ≈ $19.8 M ARR   (aggressive; category-leader territory)
```
SaaS alone caps out ~$20–40M ARR. **It's the wedge, not the prize.**

### Model B — Marketplace take-rate (the prize)
```
Group/family trips planned by a human per year (3 regions, addressable)  ~3–6 M trips   ○
Avg multi-family trip GMV (several families, multi-night)                 ~$8k–$25k      ◐ (use $12k)
Voyara take (blended: % of planner fee + booking commissions share)      ~1.5–3%        ○ (use 2%)

→ 100,000 trips/yr × $12k × 2%     ≈ $24 M
→ 300,000 trips/yr × $12k × 2%     ≈ $72 M
→ 1,000,000 trips/yr × $12k × 2%   ≈ $240 M
```
The lever that dominates is **trip volume on-platform** (liquidity), not price. Hitting 300k–1M
group trips/yr across 3 regions is the difference between a nice business and a category winner.

## 4. Regional deep-dive

### 🌎 Americas (esp. USA) — **best beachhead, start here**
- **Size/why:** deepest **advisor culture** on earth; the host-agency model (Fora, Travel Leaders,
  Dream Vacations) is booming; US leisure travel spend ~$1T+ ⬤; multigen/family travel is a top-3
  growth segment ⬤.
- **Monetization:** highest willingness-to-pay for planning; advisors comfortable with tools + fees.
- **Difficulty:** ◐ medium — competitive on advisor tooling, but the **group-expense + execution**
  wedge is open. English-first, one big homogeneous market → fastest GTM.
- **Take:** **#1 priority.** Land US independent advisors + multi-family group travelers first.

### 🌍 Europe — **large, but structurally different**
- **Size/why:** huge outbound + intra-EU travel; **package/tour-operator dominant** (TUI, DER, big
  UK/German package market) ⬤; strong family + group travel.
- **Monetization:** independent-advisor culture **thinner** than the US (more package + OTA);
  planning fees less normalized. **GDPR** raises the data bar (you're already privacy-minded — an
  asset).
- **Difficulty:** ◐–○ medium-hard — fragmented by country/language; tour operators own the group
  relationship. Best entry: **multi-family group travel** (which packages serve poorly) + the
  expense-split moat (very resonant for intra-EU friend/family trips).
- **Take:** **#2**, after a US proof point. Start UK/Ireland (English) → DACH → Nordics.

### 🌏 Asia-Pacific — **biggest growth, hardest monetization**
- **Size/why:** fastest-growing outbound (China rebound, **India multigen family travel** booming,
  SE Asia rising middle class) ⬤; group tours enormous (esp. China) ⬤; **OTA-dominant** (Trip.com,
  MakeMyTrip, Traveloka).
- **Monetization:** **lower per-trip planning fees**, price-sensitive, OTA habit is strong; but
  **volume is massive** and **multi-generational/extended-family group travel is cultural** (India
  especially → the multi-family expense-split is *highly* relevant).
- **Difficulty:** ○ hard — localization, payments, OTA gravity, trust norms differ by country.
- **Take:** **#3 / long-game.** India is the most natural first wedge (English-capable, family-group
  culture, the split-the-bill pain is acute, rising affluence). China = separate, hard, later.

| Region | Advisor culture | Group/family travel | Monetization/trip | GTM difficulty | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Americas | High ⬤ | High ⬤ | High | Medium | **1** |
| Europe | Medium ◐ | High ⬤ | Medium | Medium-hard | **2** |
| Asia-Pacific | Low–Med ◐ | Very high ⬤ | Low (volume!) | Hard | **3** |

## 5. Is it untapped? (competitive landscape — honest)

**Not virgin, but the specific wedge is open.** The space splits into players that each miss part
of the picture:

- **Advisor tooling / CRM:** TravelJoy, Travefy, Tern, Kabbage, Fora (host agency) — itineraries +
  client mgmt. **Gap:** weak on **group/multi-family execution + per-family expense split + the
  traveler's on-trip experience.** They serve the *advisor*, not the *group of travelers*.
- **OTAs:** Booking, Expedia, Trip.com, MakeMyTrip — **booking, not planning**; nothing for
  group coordination or who-owes-whom.
- **AI planners:** Layla, Mindtrip, Wonderplan, Roam Around, + every LLM — **generate** plans
  (the VC-funded crowd you flagged). **Gap:** no human trust for high-stakes group trips, no
  execution/settlement layer. (And — per your own insight — Voyara can *consume* their output via
  Smart Paste rather than compete.)
- **Group-trip / split tools:** Splitwise (expenses only), TripIt (itinerary inbox), WhatsApp
  (the actual "coordination tool" today). **Gap:** none of them plan, and none connect a planner.

**The open wedge = the intersection nobody owns:**
> **a human planner + modern execution + multi-family expense split + an on-trip companion, built
> for GROUP travel.** That intersection is Voyara's existing product strength (the moat + the
> itinerary engine + the on-trip features you just shipped). The planner is the distribution +
> trust layer on top.

**Untapped score:** ◐ **6.5/10 untapped** — enough white space to win a defensible niche, not so
empty that there's no demand proof (the adjacent players validate the demand).

## 6. Why Voyara can win this wedge (the unfair advantages)
1. **The expense-split moat** — no advisor tool or AI planner does per-family/per-room splitting.
   It's the reason a *group* adopts you, and it compounds (the settlement recap is shared to the
   other families → built-in CAC advantage, already in your roadmap §10).
2. **Execution + on-trip** — you've built the deterministic itinerary engine, timezone-aware live
   trip, packing, recap. Advisors hand clients a PDF today; you hand them a living app.
3. **AI-agnostic** — Smart Paste means you ride the AI-planner wave instead of fighting it.
4. **Privacy posture** — a real asset for Europe (GDPR) and trust-sensitive group data.

## 7. Risks / what would make the numbers NOT happen
- **Two-sided cold-start** (marketplace) — hardest part; mitigate by starting **Model A** (sell
  advisors a tool that's valuable with zero network).
- **Take-rate compression** — advisors guard their margins; the % may be lower than 2%.
- **Trust + liability** — connecting strangers to plan high-value trips needs reviews/guarantees.
- **Regional drag** — Europe/Asia each need real localization; don't model them as "US × population."
- **Incumbent response** — Fora/TravelJoy could add group-split; your moat must stay ahead.
